I've been thinking about why I dislike drinking, and I think the worst consequences don't really show up until I've been drinking regularly.

One night of drinking doesn't necessarily feel like a problem. I can have fun, enjoy myself, and wake up the next day feeling mostly fine. But string enough of those nights together and something changes.

I stop feeling present in my own life.

Alcohol feels like a time warp. A weekend disappears, then a week, then a month. I know I've been doing things, spending time with friends, having fun, but somehow I don't feel like I've actually been living those moments. I'm just along for the ride.

And while my body is spending its energy recovering from alcohol, I feel like my mind and spirit are doing the same. I'm not growing, reflecting, or really moving forward. I'm just recovering enough to do it again.

That's what makes it so insidious. The consequences don't necessarily show up after one night. They emerge after you've established a pattern.

This morning was a reminder.

My dog hurt her foot last night, and instead of waking up feeling prepared to take care of her, I woke up stressed, tired, and disappointed in myself. I'll probably skip CrossFit tonight because my body already feels like it's under enough stress.

None of these things is catastrophic. But that's kind of the point.

**One night of drinking can become the first domino in a series of choices that move me further away from the life I actually want.**

And I think that's what bothers me most.

It's not just the physical effects or the lost time. It's knowing that I'm actively choosing something that creates distance between who I am and who I want to be.

It erodes my trust in myself.

I want to be present. I want to wake up ready to deal with whatever life throws at me. I want to feel grounded, capable, and in control of my decisions.

I don't want to spend my life recovering from the way I chose to spend it.

I'm writing this because these feelings are easy to forget once I start feeling better. The disappointment fades, the appeal of drinking returns, and suddenly the consequences don't seem so significant anymore.

**I don't want to bury this feeling just because it's uncomfortable. I want to remember it, understand it, and actually learn from it.**

Because ultimately, I want to feel like the person making decisions today is looking out for the person who has to live with them tomorrow.
